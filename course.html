01

How this hacks your memory

Read once. These nine levers are wired into every single day.

RETRIEVAL Active recall beats re-reading

Every build day ends with a closed-book "Lock-in." You pull the answer from memory instead of recognising it on a page. The pulling is what builds the wire.

SPACED Callbacks on a decay curve

Day 43 asks you to recall Day 9. Day 41 resurfaces a Week-1 concept. Material comes back right as it's about to fade. Forgetting a little, then recovering, is the point.

INTERLEAVE Mixed, not blocked

Review days shuffle domains together instead of drilling one. Messier, slower, and it sticks far harder than clean repetition of a single topic.

FEYNMAN Teach it back

You're a technical writer. Your unfair advantage is explaining. Half the Lock-ins make you narrate a concept in your own words. If you can't, you didn't learn it.

ANCHOR New hung on known

You already own the AWS mental model. Every Azure concept gets pinned to its AWS twin (see the Rosetta table). You're not memorising from zero, you're mapping.

GENERATE Guess before you're told

Try the lab before the video where you can. A wrong prediction you then correct is remembered better than a right answer you were handed.

STREAK Don't break the chain

Signal lock counts your completed days. A visible streak turns "should study" into "can't lose the chain." Cheap dopamine, aimed at the exam.

SLEEP Consolidate on the pillow

Rest days say reread before sleep for a reason. Memory consolidates overnight. Light review at night + one recall at wake doubles retention for near-zero effort.

METACOG Hunt your weak spots

Every exam prompt ends with a weak-spot report. You don't study what you know, you study what the score says you don't. Confidence is a liar; the report isn't.
